PhysBox: Volt

A complete browser-native EDA & mixed-signal studio — schematic capture, XSPICE/Ngspice simulation, frequency-domain analysis, and 2-layer PCB fabrication on one canvas — powered by a synchronous WebAssembly SPICE engine with real-time Hardware-In-The-Loop (HIL) silicon and live 3D electromechanical co-simulation.

  • Transient, DC Bias & Bode Frequency Sweeps: Inspect live oscilloscope traces, toggle quiescent DC operating-point voltage chips on every net, run log-frequency Bode magnitude/phase sweeps with automatic −3 dB corner readouts, and catch overloaded resistors or reverse-biased LEDs with live ERC and power-rating checks.
  • Single & 2-Layer PCB Milling, Stencils & Gerbers: Auto-place and drag-to-reroute footprints with copper flood and pad clearance rings. Probe a 4×4 conductive heightmap directly on copper-clad FR4 over WebSerial, drill spoilboard flip-alignment pins for double-sided boards, send SMD paste stencils to Etch, or export RS-274X Gerbers.
  • Realtime HIL & Live Mesh Co-Simulation: Bridge physical ESP32/CYD microcontrollers in realtime over WebSerial, route live WebAudio mic/speaker signals through analog filters, or click Link Mesh to drive 3D NEMA 17 steppers, DC gearmotors, and acoustic speaker enclosures from real SPICE currents.

PhysBox: Mesh

Model. Simulate. Listen. Make.
A multi-domain mechanical, acoustic, and digital fabrication studio combining MuJoCo WebAssembly with Lattice cage modelling, clay sculpting, WASM OpenSCAD, and path-traced rendering. Build assemblies that collide concavely, float upright, ring at concert pitch, and export to printers, mills, lasers, and casting benches.

  • True Concave Collision, Hydrostatics, Flexures & Fracture: Automatic background V-HACD decomposition means cups hold balls and funnels feed parts with zero proxy setup. Simulate container-bounded water with 3D Archimedes righting torques, compliant leaf flexures, shearable welds, Hertzian denting/shattering, and internal M4–M16 hex-nut ballast (Stand, Spin, Float).
  • Zero-Sample 3D Vibro-Acoustics & Luthier Tuning: Coupled Kirchhoff–Love shell + 3D FDTD cavity FIR convolution, Rayleigh–Ritz modal bars, Schelleng bowed/plucked strings (4/4 cello, lyre), and 3D TMM wind/brass waveguides — carve an f-hole or thin a spruce top and hear the resonance change live.
  • Lattice CAD, Clay Sculpting, Surface Patterns & OpenSCAD: Model dimensioned hard-surface cages on a decade grid (0.1–100 mm) with live edge rounding (E), slice sculpted clay with viewport scissors, apply 7 procedural architectural patterns (kumiko, guilloché, fluted, parquet), or write parametric OpenSCAD with auto-generated UI sliders.
  • Molds, Slip-Casting, Timber Joinery & Multi-Setup CAM: Generate separable silicone/rigid pour molds with sliding C-clips, plaster slip-cast molds, PVA dissolvable liners, multi-material 3MF slicer plates, mortise-and-tenon timber breakdowns, bonded brickwork, and multi-setup CNC/relief/sheet-unfold jobs — then click Add Simulation to test the machined or cast part back in physics.

Agentic AI Tooling

Empowering AI Agents with Actionable Ground Truth — and the ability to create change in the physical world.

Bridging cognitive reasoning with physically grounded environments. physbox.io provides zero-latency local action spaces where agents can test layouts, solve physical problems, and safely interact with real-world machines.

Action Spaces Over Empty Logic

Large Language Models reason through text and code, but they operate in a physical vacuum. Without realistic feedback, an AI agent cannot correctly optimize an audio amplifier circuit, balance an unstable mechanical linkage, test a physical ESP32 HIL loop, or generate printable 3D CAD parts. They need grounded world models to solve complex, multi-modal tasks.

PhysBox solves this by exposing high-accuracy physical, hardware, and fabrication engines directly to AI agents. It provides a robust, local sandbox where agents can explore bounds, isolate failure points, and solve complex spatial problems.

Native Interfaces for Agents

Built entirely on client-side WebAssembly, our simulators enable AI agents to bridge the gap between abstract reasoning and concrete physical execution. Every engine ships a first-class agent interface over the Model Context Protocol:

  • Native MCP Server, Not Brittle Scraping: physbox-mcp exposes the simulators as 259 typed Model Context Protocol tools over a local WebSocket relay. Agents call physics_build_scene or circuit_run_sim directly.
  • Procedural CAD & 3D Printing Generation: AI agents write and refactor OpenSCAD code to create custom brackets, gears, and enclosures, test structural stability in MuJoCo, and output 3D-printable STL files.
  • Real-World Hardware & Telemetry (HIL): Using WebSocket telemetry, serial HIL bridges, and general IoT webhooks, agents stream hardware pin states, read sensor values, and drive physical microcontrollers directly.
  • Iterative Design Self-Correction: Agents observe physical bounds and signal waveforms at 60fps, self-correcting schematic nets and CAD parameters until physical and functional requirements are met.
Model Context Protocol

259 Tools. One pip install.

PhysBox: MCP is a companion server that puts every simulator on the other end of a tool call. Commands travel as JSON over a local WebSocket relay straight into the running browser tab (or native desktop window). Two hundred and forty-five are simulator tools; ten more read the run archive and your account; four — detect_apps, send_command, list_sessions and use_session — work across all of them.

Connect in Two Steps

1

Install the companion server

pip install physbox-mcp
2

Register it with your MCP client

Drop this into .mcp.json for Claude Code, or ~/.gemini/config/mcp_config.json for Gemini in Antigravity.

{
  "mcpServers": {
    "physbox-mcp": {
      "type": "stdio",
      "command": "physbox-mcp",
      "args": ["--stdio"]
    }
  }
}

Open volt, mesh, or etch.physbox.io and the page registers itself with the relay on load — sending live heartbeats during heavy CAM/CSG builds so long tool calls never time out.

Works across multiple open tabs simultaneously — keep Volt, Mesh, and Etch (or several scenes of the same studio) open side by side and your agent addresses each tab cleanly by session without cross-talk.

The Closed Loop

An agent designing a printable, cast, or CNC-machined assembly doesn't just emit geometry and hope. It runs the full physical cycle, and the simulator gives ground-truth feedback:

  1. physics_create_lattice / physics_validate_scad Build dimensioned cage geometry or compile OpenSCAD, inspect real-world bounding boxes, and run physics_check_dfm to catch thin walls or steep overhangs early.
  2. physics_build_scene & physics_run_headless Drop the assembly into MuJoCo with joints, flexures, water buoyancy, or coupled Volt motors, and step N ticks to verify nothing binds or tips.
  3. physics_make_pour_box / physics_export_cut Generate separable C-clip molds, slip-cast plaster shells, timber mortise-and-tenon setups, or hand unfolded finger-joint sheets straight to Etch.
  4. physics_get_telemetry / physics_get_screenshot Read exact joint/contact numbers or capture a clean, overlay-free viewport frame (clean: true) for visual inspection.
  5. Correct and repeat. The mold drafts cleanly, the linkage stops binding, the filter hits −3 dB on target — because physics, not model confidence, grades the work.

Core Strengths

  • Zero-friction browser access with coupled SPICE, MuJoCo, hydrostatics, and 3D vibro-acoustics.
  • 259 native MCP tools built as closed-loop physical world models for AI agents.
  • End-to-end fabrication: multi-material 3MF, silicone/slip-cast molds, 2-layer PCBs, timber joinery, and laser/CNC G-code.
  • 100% local WASM execution with no account required (plus optional C++20 desktop builds for heavy workloads).

Honest Tradeoffs & Limitations

  • Browser tabs face a 4GB WebAssembly heap cap (solved on desktop by PhysBox Native C++20).
  • Subtractive CAM targets 2.5D, 3D relief, and indexed multi-face setups rather than continuous 5-axis simultaneous milling.
  • Direct USB WebSerial streaming requires a Chromium-based browser (Chrome, Edge, Brave, Opera) or PhysBox Native.
